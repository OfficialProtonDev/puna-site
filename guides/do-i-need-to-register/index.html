Do I need to register my water supply?

Updated 24 Sept 2026

If you supply drinking water to anyone other than your own household, you are probably a drinking water supplier under the Water Services Act 2021 – even if it's just a tank on the wharekai roof or a bore that feeds a café.

The law changed on 26 August 2025. Here is where things stand now.

Who doesn't need to register

  • Household supplies serving 25 people or fewer are no longer treated as drinking water supplies. That covers a house on its own roof tank, or a few homes sharing a bore, as long as they're only used as homes.
  • Suppliers who were registered with 25 or fewer household users can ask Taumata Arowai to de-register.

Who does need to register – whatever their size

  • Marae, papakāinga with community buildings, kura, kōhanga reo and schools with their own water.
  • Community halls, churches and sports clubs.
  • Businesses: cafés, restaurants, wineries, campgrounds, lodges, B&Bs, farmstays, workplaces.
  • Rural water schemes and shared supplies serving more than 25 people.

The deadlines

  • Existing supplies that were operating before 15 November 2021 but aren't registered have until November 2028 to register.
  • Those supplies then have until November 2030 to meet the rest of their legal obligations.
  • The duty to supply safe drinking water applies now, whatever your registration status.
  • New supplies (started after 15 November 2021) must be registered before they supply water.
  • Registration must now be renewed every five years (it used to be every year).

How to register

You register online in Hinekōrako, Taumata Arowai's portal, using a RealMe login. You'll be asked about your supply's size and location, where the water comes from, how it's treated and distributed, and who is responsible for each part.

Puna's free supply check works out whether you need to register, and once you sign up it produces a one-page registration summary with the answers Hinekōrako asks for.
