Drinking water rules for marae

Updated 24 Sept 2026

Marae are community buildings, so the 25-person household exemption doesn't apply. If your marae supplies its own drinking water – from roof tanks, a bore, a spring or a stream – it's a drinking water supply that needs to be registered by November 2028 and fully compliant by November 2030.

The simplest way to comply

Most marae can use the Self-supplied Buildings Acceptable Solution: a cartridge filter and a validated UV unit on the water going to the wharekai, wharenui and ablutions, regular tests at an accredited lab, tank checks, and simple records. No safety plan and no compliance reports.

If your marae normally has 25 people or fewer, the Very Small Communities rules are even lighter on paper: E. coli and coliform tests every six months, with extra treatment and testing before big events. But you still have to make sure the water is safe, so filtration and UV are strongly recommended.

Planning for tangihanga and hui

Busy periods matter. Under the Acceptable Solution, if a marae that normally serves up to 100 people has more than 100, treated water must be tested in the week before (if the event is planned) and weekly while numbers stay high. Make sure your UV unit is sized for peak flow.

Support

Government programmes have funded drinking water upgrades for marae, and Taumata Arowai has run workshops on marae. Puna offers community pricing for marae, and its records and plans make funding applications and reporting easier.
